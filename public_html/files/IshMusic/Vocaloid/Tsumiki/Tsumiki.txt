Tsumiki (ツミキ) is a japanese vocaloid producer. 

Their music trends towards rock, but with high bpms and extreme guitar/drums/bass usage. A lot of jazz elements are also present in their music.

Other instruments typically employed in their music is a synthesizer, various sound effects, and a unique piano roll. Their piano rolls are just intense and fast as as rest of the song, with many notes sounding dissonant and jumpy, nearly jazz-like. 

Like a lot of producers in this genre, the music they make are extremely intense, busy, and jumpy. They employ an enormous amount of stops and mutes to build up to thes sensation of euphoria/urge to headbang. This combined with the high pitched and scratchy voice banks/singer make for a unique listening experience with a lot to pay attention to.

Tsumiki's first song establishes the bases for the rest of their songs, with a formula that is somewhat followed for every other song. 

Song Structure Breakdown:

Intro-
Tsumiki songs have a long intro that can be identified in two parts. 

1) a less intense/minor chording that builds/leads into the main progression/key of the song. typically very short part that can be characterized with only one or two aspects in it, which juxtaposes the rest of the song.

2) a pure instrumental that sometimes includes scatting that sets up the tempo/beat/motif of the song. different guitars can be heard, the drumming is intense, and bass is present. in some songs, the main motif is set up with a synth. the same drum beat is carried into the verse

2.9) how they bridge the intro the verse is always seamless, adding in the vocals to continue the riff the guitars have going on, as if it was another instrument (which vocals are) continuing the intro. they fade out one or two instruments to give the verse a distinct difference of lesser intensity.

Verse-

In General- broken up into two parts clearly by a halt in the voicebank and extra instrumentals for a varying amount of time, then returns to the same tune, with varying differences to an addition of an extra layer, or goes up a whole intensity level with the instrumentation. there is a difference- its just like a little intermission. The instrumentation stays at a constant voicing/chord, and does not enhibit any chord progression.

Drums- as much as i love them, they're nothing too special. they're always there and don't really change the rudiments unless its at transitions, whcih you can clearly hear a fill, and some hi hats, but nothing else.

Bass- also a huge fan of it, it enchances any tune/song. but nothing special in this case, it follows a strict beat that follows the singer's. its definitely funky. it gives it more of that 'scat' sound from how it is played. its percicse with little continuity so it sounds jumpy- which goes with tsumiki's style.

The rest of it- its unique for every song so i will generalize it here. there is a complete absence of chord progression in all of the instrumentation. Different instruments are constantly overlayed with different riffs/rolls/tunes, seemingly dissonant. and not matching the chording for the vocals at all, except for its bpm and cadence. These layers/instruments range from the piano, guitar, synths, and sound effects. There is a dynamic air to them as well, as they switch to the L and R audios, giving it this continued sense of the layer coming from seemingly nowhere. 

Tid-bits-
