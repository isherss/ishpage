keyboardAbout

Hi webpage browser! In here I have compiled a bunch of information about my mechanical keyboard escapades through the years.

My Journey started in July 2020, when a friend convinced me to try out the Ducky One 2 Mini, a 60%. I was still playing on a laptop at the time and I exclusively played Destiny 2 which would make that thing run HOT. Instead of continuously burning my fingers, I finally got an external keyboard. Why did I choose a 60% for my first keyboard? I don't know and will never know. I think I'm a sucker for making my life harder.

Later in the year, I started planning out my first ever PC build. I camped out a Microcenter with a friend at 3am in December. It was cold. According to the historical records, it was 32°F out that morning. I remember playing proseka on our phones on a folding chair to pass time but we could only play 3 songs before our hands got too stiff to actually play anything. My dad sat in the car eyeing us and I still do not know why we didn't just take turns sitting in the warm car. But it was worth it- we got lucky that day and there were 12 3080s in stock! We were 3rd in line, so I snagged an EVGA FTW3. It still sits in my computer as we speak.

But I digress, becasue of the new computer build I believed I needed a new keyboard. The GMMK Pro just came out and it was a hell of an upgrade from my Ducky One 2 Mini. So I got one. It was a 75% TKL so it was a tad bigger than I was used to. I liked it a lot. It was the first time I 'put a keyboard together' instead of just having a prebuilt and building it was soooo fun. Even if all the parts came with it and it was hotswap. I spent a lot of time 'customizing' and I used it for a while.

Until I came across kbdfans. I was into retro stuff. I'm still into retro stuff. I wanted a retro keyboard. So I got one. With Jade Greens. Oh my god is that thing so loud and clicky. I bought my first keycap set ever, KAT Space Cadet. Cause yes, I thought (and still think) the Space Cadet keyboard was the peak of keyboards. That was my first time soldering. I didn't want to get a hotswap pcb this time beacuse I was cheap and also... soldering is cool. So I put that together and it was awesome. It's no longer assembled today unfortunately but the parts are in a different keyboard. The KAT Space Cadet sits on my Alpha Alpha 35. Looking real cool.

Now, my first ever FULL keyboard build. the Nibble 65. In hindsight, it was a bad idea to start with this. Especially when I meant it as a gift for someone at the time. I feel like as a gift, I pumped out something that could've been much much better after a bit more experience... but I am saying that 6 year later now. Anyway, I was so proud of this build. I spent a LOT of time on it. Soldered every diode, every switch, I added a little OLED screen, a rotary knob, LEDs, the whole schebang. It was awesome. I still think its awesome. I think the person I gifted it to at the time thought it was awesome. But I should have definetly soldered those joints tighter. It was my second time soldering and I sucked. And I gifted that, oh my god it feels cringe looking back on it. Well, the stuff I build now doesn't fall apart so I can relish in that.

Now I'm discovering DIY kits. Lily 58 was my second. The pipeline into 40%s start. 58 keys?? But it was ergonomic. And I like suffering. I soldered the microcontrollers and the diodes and switched and tsrrs connectors and... well I never ended up using it because although I like suffering I could never figure out how to use keyboard layouts without the default stagger. It was weird. I even designed an ortho keyoboard in solidworks I dubbed 'ishBoard' and looking back it, I lol. I cannot use ortho or 'ergonomic' layouts for the life of me.

One of my favorite keyboard designs at the time was the wilba.tech Thermal. A HBBK 60% that is designed after a heatsink essentially, I don't know why but I was seriously attracted to it. The industrial design, the simplicity... I just love things that give me that RAW technology feel. To the roots. Nothing flashy, just plain functionality. I forever stand by that the old webpages and UIs of the past cannot be improved on. I still prefer working on code in a notepad, I ran vim for a while on my archlinux installation, I open everything through terminals... in university whenever we had to code in python, R, or anything, I'd use a text editor and the terminal to compile and run my code. Simple. I hate how bloated everything is today. Anyway, I digress again...

I tried again to design a keyboard in solidworks. It took inspiration from the wilba.tech Thermal design but used heat PIPES instead. It was pretty cool, but also I didn't know how to get things manufactured and I was still ass at designing keyboard layouts so... nothing really came of it.

Anyway, around this time, I was super active in the keyboard community. From mid 2021-mid 2023 you could find me in every single keyboard discord server, local keyboard meetup, you name it. I got introduced to the minivan in early 2022. A 40% that was pretty universal- there were lots of cases for it, lots of different pcb design and layouts for it, it felt like a pretty easy way to get into 40% without the huge cost hurdle or aesthetic sacrifices. I got a basic plastic 40%. I still have the build. It has some of my favorite switches ever in them, mid-profile GMX Browns. I actually used them in the current keyboard (any favorite keyboard) I am using right now. Its the perfect blend of clicky and tactile. In fact, it has a click leaf that is dampened to differentiate it from an actual clicky switch. Anyway, digressing again...

Around the same time, I was using different keyboard layouts. I was learning colemak. I'm no programmer so I didn't see the benefits in Dvorak and so Colemak seemed like the most reasonable one for me, just trying to be efficient and more ergonomic. I'm by no means an ultra fast typer (150wpm on Monkeytype - english - stop on word or 100 wpm on Monkeytype - english 5k - stop on word) but oh man was Colemak a doozy to learn. At some point, a friend of mine showed me there was a keyboard typing tournement going on for a new keyboard layout that the people of the alternative keyboard layout community put together. The layout is called Canary. It was made to emphasize the rolling motion turing typing. It was honestly really similar to Colemak. I think only 5 keys or so were different. So I said, fuck it we ball and spent 2 weeks trying to get as fast as possible on the new layout. By the end of the tournament I could hit 75wpm on english 5k. The winner hit more than 150wpm. Woof. I wasn't actually trying to win but actually meeting these crazy fast typers on a whole new keyboard layout in TWO WEEKS was insane. I still did way better than my friend though, who could only hit 40wpm coming from qwerty. Which makes sense, I don't think theres a single letter in the same spot as the qwerty layout.

Anyway, after this I started really pushing the limits of how small I could go. I was really good at learning this. Not ortho, but this. Tap dances, combos, layers, mod taps, I got to learn everything that QMK had to offer. The smallest I ever got to use for a while was the Alpha 29, which for some reason I can't find online anymore but it was essentially the alpha 28 but with a spacebar on the fourth row. i can't deal with a 3 row keyboard. It hurts my thumb to move it so far up, my thumb naturally rests on the fourth row. Even though its aura, I'm not willing to give up my ergonomics and comfort for something.

Which now you may ask, isn't using a 30% uncomfortable? Which I would tell you no, I could never ever go back to a larger keyboard anymore because of how uncomfortable it is. My fingers were not built to stretch so far to hit any of the top row numbers. I use numbers often. Now I have all of my numbers either in my homerow or the row above. So comfortable. My backspace still feels like a stretch but it is literally a key above my pinky. It is not comfortable to stretch your fingers up the keyboard. Everything I need is on my keyboard and a single key away. Can't beat that.
